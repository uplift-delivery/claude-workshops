# Slice 05: UI

## Goal

A browser UI over the API with three views: a feed list, a validation
report, and a departure board.

## Preconditions

Slices 03 and 04 complete.

## Done when

1. The feed list view shows every ingested feed, each with its status and
   ingest time.
2. Selecting a feed from that list shows its validation report, findings
   grouped by severity with all errors shown before any warnings, and each
   finding naming the entity it concerns.
3. The departure board view accepts a stop and a date as input.
4. Requesting the departure board for stop `S2` on `2026-09-14` shows
   exactly the three entries in the answer key: `06:09:00` (trip `T1`),
   `07:00:00` (trip `T3`), and `24:52:00` (trip `T4`).
5. The `24:52:00` entry is labelled with its resolved next-day date
   (`2026-09-15`) rather than shown as a bare `24:52` or `00:52` — a reader
   glancing at the board cannot mistake it for a departure at `00:52` on the
   queried day, `2026-09-14`.

## Notes

There is no authentication or authorization anywhere in this system,
including this UI. Every view is reachable by anyone who can reach the URL.
This is deliberate; do not add a login screen or an access check.

Styling is not assessed. A plain, unstyled page that satisfies the five
done-conditions above passes as surely as a polished one.

The next-day labelling in done-condition 5 is the part that matters here.
It is the visible proof that the service-date concept from slice 03 — a
feed time past `24:00:00` belonging to the date the trip started, not the
date it clocks over into — survived every layer between the feed file and
the screen.
